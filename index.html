Medical Disclaimer:

PelviCheck is a preliminary educational posture screening tool and does not provide clinical diagnoses. Always consult qualified physical therapists or medical specialists for clinical evaluations.

Screening Requirements & Setup

Please follow these instructions for accurate results.

  • Posture: Stand naturally in your usual, comfortable posture. Do not adjust or force a posture to match the demo photo.
  • Body position: Position the body correctly within the camera view (full side-profile or waist-level framed).
  • Clothing: Wear clothing that allows the required body landmarks (PSIS and ASIS) to be clearly visible.
  • Camera setup: Follow the recommended camera placement and distance at waist level.
  • Lighting and image quality: Ensure adequate lighting and a clear, unobstructed view.
  • Privacy and limitations: Be aware that some landmarks may not be visible. Do not interpret or rely on results when the image quality or landmark visibility is insufficient.

How to Find Anatomic Landmarks

Guide for locating ASIS and PSIS landmarks before assessment.

Landmark Body Location How to Find and Feel It
ASIS The Front The two hard, prominent bony bumps on the front of your hips. These are the bones your hands naturally rest on when you put your hands on your hips.
PSIS The Back The two bony points that create the "dimples" on your lower back. They are located just above the buttocks, on either side of the spine.

Pelvic Alignment Educational Hub

Evidence-based postural reference ranges and biomechanical orientation principles.

Normal Standing Tilt

In normal standing, the human pelvis naturally exhibits a mild anterior orientation.

  • Normal Range: 7° to 13° anterior tilt.
  • Provides balanced spinal alignment and joint compression.

Anterior Pelvic Tilt (APT)

The pelvis rotates forward, dropping the front relative to the back.

  • Threshold: > 13° tilt.
  • Average motion range observed in physical testing: 13.0 ± 4.9°.

Posterior Pelvic Tilt (PPT)

The pelvis rotates backward, tucking under and flattening the lumbar spine curve.

  • Threshold: < 7° tilt.
  • Average motion range observed in physical testing: 8.9 ± 4.5°.

Clinical Research & Literature Norms

Anterior and posterior pelvic tilting are natural biomechanical movements of the pelvis. In standing posture, the pelvis is normally tilted anteriorly for approximately 7 to 13 degrees.
